# English style guide — CHARM VILLA

Status: approved for working use (2026-10-01).

## Voice
Literary and restrained, as in the Chinese: concrete observation of material, light, texture and gesture rather than
evaluative adjectives. Calm declarative sentences. The brand does not exclaim, does not hurry the reader and does not
promise outcomes.

- Prefer: *grain, plait, outline, fold, lid, weave, surface, proportion, the hand, light catches, rests, falls, unfurls*.
- Avoid: *luxurious, exquisite, stunning, perfect, premium, must-have, elevate, indulge, curated, timeless*, exclamation
  marks, rhetorical questions, superlatives, "you deserve".
- Person: mostly impersonal or second person implied ("Look first at…"). "We/our" only for stores and service text.
- Product language: a tea bag is a *goldfish* (count: "six goldfish"), a work is a *piece* or *object*.

## Register by surface
- Headlines and story titles: sentence case, no full stop unless two sentences. Latin display labels that are already
  uppercase in the design (eyebrows such as `KEEP EXPLORING`) stay uppercase.
- Body: full sentences, one idea each. Chinese paired clauses joined by 「，」 usually become two sentences or one
  sentence with a semicolon or colon. No em dashes.
- Calls to action: plain verb + object ("View gift box", "Find a store", "Add to bag"). They must name the real next
  step; the cart is called *bag*.
- Forms, errors, status: short, neutral, polite; no blame.
- Alt text: describe what is visible, product first, no "image of".

## Mechanics
- Numbers, units and dimensions exactly as the source (`28.6 × 11.7 × 9 cm`, `NT$ 2,000`).
- Dates: `Aug 11`, `Oct 3`; times `10:00–21:00`.
- Counts of tea bags: "12 per box", "3 × Taiwan Rose Oolong Tea".
- Separator in product names: Chinese 「｜」 becomes ` | `, 「・」 becomes ` · `.
- No mixed language in English pages except protected terms and proper names.
